# AV City Launch Playbook (AVCLP)

> **What if local partnership became part of the AV deployment model?**
>
> ## Live Interactive Demonstration

**[Launch the Redwood Metro Market Readiness Dashboard](https://sorianic.github.io/AV-City-Launch-Playbook/prototype/)**

Explore a synthetic AV market-entry scenario from initial discovery through final launch verification. The interactive dashboard demonstrates readiness scoring across seven domains, active risks and mitigations, and AVCLP's proposed GO / CONDITIONAL GO / NO-GO decision logic.

**Scenario progression:** T-180 Baseline → T-60 Review → T-30 Verification

AV City Launch Playbook (AVCLP) is an independent, open framework exploring how autonomous vehicle operators could approach new-market entry as both an operational deployment and an opportunity to build a stronger local ecosystem.

AV companies already coordinate with cities, regulators, first responders, community organizations, infrastructure providers, and other stakeholders when entering new markets.

AVCLP asks a slightly different question:

**What could happen if those individual efforts were intentionally connected into a repeatable market-entry framework?**

The goal is not simply to prepare an AV fleet for a city.

The goal is to explore how the operator, city, workforce, students, public-safety agencies, accessibility organizations, local businesses, and community could all be better prepared because the deployment is happening.

---

## The Idea

A successful AV launch requires more than vehicles.

It requires an ecosystem.

AVCLP organizes that ecosystem around eight stakeholder groups:

- AV Operator
- Government & Transportation Agencies
- Public Safety
- Universities
- Community Colleges & Workforce Partners
- Accessibility & Community Organizations
- Local Businesses
- Technology & Infrastructure Partners

Rather than treating these relationships as separate activities, AVCLP explores how they could become part of a coordinated city-entry strategy.

---

## The Framework

AVCLP uses a seven-phase lifecycle:

| Phase | Core Question | Primary Output |
|---|---|---|
| **1. DISCOVER** | Should we enter this city? | City Opportunity & Risk Assessment |
| **2. MAP** | Who needs to be involved? | Local Ecosystem Map |
| **3. PARTNER** | How can deployment create mutual value? | Partnership Action Plan |
| **4. INTRODUCE** | How should the technology meet the community? | 180-Day Community Introduction Plan |
| **5. VERIFY** | Are the operator and local ecosystem ready? | City Launch Readiness Scorecard |
| **6. DEPLOY** | How do we introduce service deliberately? | Launch Operations Dashboard |
| **7. MEASURE** | Did deployment create the expected value? | 90-Day Market Integration Review |

**Deployment is not the finish line.**

It is the point where planning assumptions begin meeting real-world operations.

---

## 1. DISCOVER

### Should we enter this city?

Evaluate the potential market before deployment planning begins.

Areas of consideration may include:

- Mobility needs and transportation gaps
- Operating environment
- Road and infrastructure complexity
- Workforce availability
- Accessibility needs
- Community considerations
- Public-safety environment
- Transit connections
- Potential local partners
- Deployment risks

**Output:** City Opportunity & Risk Assessment

---

## 2. MAP

### Who needs to be involved?

Identify organizations that could influence deployment success or benefit from participation.

Stakeholders may be classified as:

- Critical Partner
- Strategic Partner
- Community Partner
- Informational Stakeholder

The goal is to understand the local ecosystem before trying to operate within it.

**Output:** Local Ecosystem Map

---

## 3. PARTNER

### How can deployment create mutual value?

Define what participating organizations could contribute and what value they could receive.

Potential opportunities include:

- Student research
- Internships
- Technical workforce pathways
- First-responder preparation
- Accessibility testing
- Local supplier opportunities
- Transportation integration
- Community education
- Infrastructure collaboration

A partnership should have a purpose, owner, expected outcome, and measurable value.

**Output:** Partnership Action Plan

---

## 4. INTRODUCE

### How should the technology meet the community?

Community introduction begins before commercial passenger service.

Potential activities include:

- Open houses
- Vehicle demonstrations
- University events
- Student programs
- First-responder exercises
- Accessibility workshops
- Local supplier sessions
- Community Q&As

The objective is not simply to promote autonomous vehicles.

The objective is to give people opportunities to understand the technology, experience it, question it, provide feedback, and participate in how it enters their community.

**Output:** 180-Day Community Introduction Plan

---

## 5. VERIFY READINESS

### Are the operator and local ecosystem actually ready?

AVCLP evaluates seven proposed readiness domains:

| Domain | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Operational** | Fleet, facilities, processes, and support |
| **Public Safety** | Training, procedures, and coordination |
| **Workforce** | Staffing, training, and qualification |
| **Community** | Engagement and unresolved concerns |
| **Accessibility** | Testing and stakeholder feedback |
| **Infrastructure** | Mapping, connectivity, and facilities |
| **Partnerships** | Required relationships and actions |

Example readiness thresholds:

- **GREEN:** 85–100
- **WATCH:** 75–84
- **MITIGATION REQUIRED:** Below 75

Safety-critical domains could require higher minimum thresholds.

A high overall average should never hide a critical readiness gap.

**Output:** City Launch Readiness Scorecard

---

## 6. DEPLOY

### How do we introduce service deliberately?

Deployment is treated as an operational phase rather than the end of the project.

Potential indicators include:

- Fleet availability
- Service reliability
- Operational incidents
- Emergency interactions
- Remote-support events
- Accessibility issues
- Rider feedback
- Workforce performance
- Community concerns

Deployment could occur through controlled expansion waves rather than immediate full-scale service.

**Output:** Launch Operations Dashboard

---

## 7. MEASURE

### Did the deployment create the expected value?

Conduct structured reviews at:

**+30 Days → +60 Days → +90 Days**

Operational measurements can be evaluated alongside local impact.

Examples include:

- Local employees hired
- Students participating
- Technical certifications completed
- First responders trained
- Accessibility recommendations implemented
- Local suppliers participating
- Community-event attendance
- Mobility gaps served
- Operational performance
- Unresolved community issues

**Output:** 90-Day Market Integration Review

# Demonstration Scenario: Redwood Metro

AVCLP includes a completely fictional demonstration city used to test and illustrate the framework.

## Redwood Metro, California

**Scenario:** RM-01  
**Status:** Synthetic / Fictional  
**Target Launch:** October 2027

| Attribute | Demonstration Data |
|---|---|
| Metro Population | 650,000 |
| Initial AV Fleet | 150 |
| Initial Service Area | 75 sq. miles |
| Universities | 2 |
| Community Colleges | 3 |
| Public-Safety Agencies | 6 |
| Major Hospitals | 4 |
| Airport | 1 Regional Airport |
| Transit | Bus + Commuter Rail |
| Existing AV Experience | Limited |
| Deployment | Phased Commercial Service |

### Mission

Prepare Redwood Metro for commercial autonomous passenger service while exploring how deployment could create measurable value for the operator, workforce, students, public-safety agencies, businesses, accessibility organizations, and community.

---

## Redwood Metro Readiness Example

### T-180 Days

| Domain | Readiness |
|---|---:|
| Operational | 68 |
| Infrastructure | 72 |
| Public Safety | 41 |
| Workforce | 38 |
| Community | 46 |
| Accessibility | 43 |
| Partnerships | 35 |

**Status: NOT LAUNCH READY**

AVCLP then applies the framework across the six months preceding deployment.

### T-30 Days

| Domain | Readiness |
|---|---:|
| Operational | 96 |
| Infrastructure | 94 |
| Public Safety | 91 |
| Workforce | 86 |
| Community | 84 |
| Accessibility | 90 |
| Partnerships | 92 |

**Demonstration Recommendation: CONDITIONAL GO**

Community readiness remains within the WATCH range, requiring continued engagement through deployment.

The purpose of the scenario is to demonstrate that readiness assessment should identify weaknesses rather than simply produce a favorable overall score.

# Creating Local Value

AVCLP explores whether AV market entry can produce benefits beyond transportation service itself.

### Students
Exposure to autonomous systems, applied research, internships, projects, and emerging career fields.

### Workforce
New technical, fleet, operations, support, and infrastructure career pathways.

### Public Safety
Greater familiarity with autonomous vehicles before emergency interactions occur.

### Accessibility
Earlier participation in determining how autonomous mobility can better serve people with disabilities and other mobility needs.

### Local Businesses
Potential participation in facilities, maintenance, charging, towing, logistics, and operational support.

### Community
Earlier opportunities to understand the technology, ask questions, provide feedback, and participate in the deployment process.

### AV Operator
Stronger local relationships, better preparedness, workforce development, operational awareness, and potentially greater community trust.
