# AV City Launch Playbook

**AVCLP v0.1**

> A proposed framework for approaching autonomous vehicle market entry through operational readiness, local partnership, workforce development, public safety, accessibility, and community engagement.

---

## Purpose

The AV City Launch Playbook provides a structured way to explore a new autonomous vehicle market from initial evaluation through post-launch review.

It is built around a simple idea:

**A city launch can be more than the introduction of autonomous vehicles. It can also become an opportunity to build local capability around the technology.**

AVCLP does not attempt to reproduce the deployment process of any existing autonomous vehicle operator.

Instead, it provides an independent framework that can be used to ask:

- Is the market operationally ready?
- Who should be involved before launch?
- Where could partnerships create mutual value?
- Are public-safety agencies prepared?
- Is a local workforce pipeline developing?
- Have accessibility needs been considered?
- Has the community had meaningful opportunities to interact with the technology?
- What should prevent or delay launch?
- How will success be measured after deployment?

---

# 1. Operating Model

AVCLP follows seven phases:

```text
DISCOVER
   ↓
MAP
   ↓
PARTNER
   ↓
INTRODUCE
   ↓
VERIFY
   ↓
DEPLOY
   ↓
MEASURE
   ↓
IMPROVE
   ↺
```

Each phase produces a defined output.

| Phase | Primary Output |
|---|---|
| DISCOVER | City Opportunity & Risk Assessment |
| MAP | Local Ecosystem Map |
| PARTNER | Partnership Action Plan |
| INTRODUCE | 180-Day Community Introduction Plan |
| VERIFY | City Launch Readiness Scorecard |
| DEPLOY | Launch Operations Dashboard |
| MEASURE | 90-Day Market Integration Review |

---

# 2. Launch Timeline

AVCLP uses a six-month pre-launch planning window for the demonstration model.

The exact timeline would depend on the market, regulatory environment, operator, and deployment.

```text

  │           │           │           │           │          │           │
DISCOVER ──> MAP ──> PARTNER ──> INTRODUCE ──> REVIEW ──> VERIFY ──> DEPLOY
                                                                          │

                                                                          │
                                                                       MEASURE
                                                                          │
                                                                       IMPROVE
```

# 3. T-180 — DISCOVER

## Objective

Determine whether the proposed market presents a viable opportunity and identify major risks before deeper launch planning begins.

This phase should establish a baseline rather than justify a decision that has already been made.

## Assessment Areas

### Mobility

Evaluate:

- Existing transportation options
- First/last-mile gaps
- Airport connectivity
- Transit connections
- Employment centers
- Major destinations
- Underserved transportation areas
- Potential service demand

### Operating Environment

Evaluate:

- Road network complexity
- Construction patterns
- Weather
- Special events
- Traffic patterns
- Service-area boundaries
- Pick-up/drop-off considerations
- Infrastructure limitations

### Workforce

Evaluate:

- Available technical workforce
- Local colleges and universities
- Existing automotive/EV programs
- Fleet operations talent
- Training infrastructure
- Potential workforce gaps

### Public Safety

Identify:

- Police departments
- Fire departments
- EMS providers
- Emergency communications/dispatch
- Towing/recovery considerations
- Existing AV familiarity
- Potential training requirements

### Accessibility

Evaluate:

- Local accessibility organizations
- Mobility needs
- Existing transportation barriers
- Potential service-design considerations
- Opportunities for early user feedback

### Community

Evaluate:

- General familiarity with AV technology
- Major community organizations
- Neighborhood groups
- Potential concerns
- Public information needs
- Opportunities for engagement

### Institutional Environment

Identify:

- City government
- Transportation agencies
- Transit providers
- Universities
- Community colleges
- Regional planning organizations
- Infrastructure partners
- Other relevant stakeholders

# 5. T-120 — PARTNER

## Objective

Convert stakeholder identification into specific, useful relationships.

AVCLP treats a partnership as an operational relationship with:

**Purpose + Contribution + Benefit + Owner + Deliverable + Measurement**

A logo on a press release is not enough.

---

## Partnership Canvas

For each proposed partnership, answer:

### Problem

What deployment or community problem are we trying to address?

### Partner

Why is this organization positioned to help?

### Operator Contribution

What could the AV operator provide?

### Partner Contribution

What could the partner provide?

### Community Value

Who benefits locally?

### Operator Value

How does the relationship improve deployment?

### Deliverable

What should actually exist because of the partnership?

### Measurement

How will we know whether it worked?

# 8. Readiness Scoring

AVCLP uses a proposed 0–100 readiness scale.

### GREEN — 85 to 100

Area is considered ready for the current deployment phase.

### WATCH — 75 to 84

Deployment may continue, but identified issues require active management.

### MITIGATION REQUIRED — Below 75

Significant gaps remain.

---

## Important Rule

**Readiness should not be determined by average score alone.**

Example:

```text
Operational        96
Infrastructure     94
Public Safety      91
Workforce          86
Community          84
Accessibility      90
Partnerships       92
```

Average readiness is high.

However:

**Community = WATCH**

That issue should remain visible rather than disappearing inside an overall score.

Safety-critical domains could also have mandatory minimum thresholds regardless of overall readiness.

# 10. T-30 — FINAL VERIFICATION

## Objective

Determine whether the market should proceed into commercial deployment.

Final review should evaluate:

- Readiness scores
- Critical risks
- Outstanding mitigations
- Public-safety preparation
- Workforce readiness
- Accessibility concerns
- Infrastructure
- Operational support
- Community concerns
- Critical partner dependencies

---

## Launch Recommendations

### GO

Required readiness gates are satisfied and no unresolved issue prevents deployment.

### CONDITIONAL GO

Deployment may proceed with defined limitations, monitoring, or mitigation requirements.

Examples:

- Reduced initial fleet
- Smaller service area
- Continued community engagement
- Additional workforce support
- Enhanced monitoring

### NO-GO

One or more unresolved conditions create unacceptable deployment risk.

A NO-GO decision should not be treated as program failure.

It means the readiness process worked.

# 11. DEPLOY

## Objective

Introduce commercial service through controlled operational expansion.

AVCLP does not assume the entire planned fleet must enter service simultaneously.

A demonstration deployment could use:

```text
WAVE 1
25 Vehicles
   ↓
REVIEW
   ↓
WAVE 2
50 Vehicles
   ↓
REVIEW
   ↓
WAVE 3
100 Vehicles
   ↓
REVIEW
   ↓

```

Expansion decisions should depend on performance rather than calendar dates alone.

# 13. 90-Day Market Integration Review

At +90 days, conduct a formal review.

## Questions

### Operations

What worked?

What failed?

What required more support than expected?

### Public Safety

Were emergency interactions handled as planned?

What procedures need improvement?

### Workforce

Was staffing sufficient?

Where did skill gaps emerge?

### Accessibility

What barriers appeared after real-world use?

What should change?

### Community

What concerns remained after launch?

Did new concerns emerge?

### Partnerships

Which partnerships created measurable value?

Which did not?

### Expansion

Is the market ready for:

- More vehicles?
- Larger service area?
- Additional operating hours?
- New service types?
- Continued hold?

# 14. Improve

AVCLP is designed as a loop.

```text
DEPLOY
   ↓
OBSERVE
   ↓
MEASURE
   ↓
IDENTIFY GAP
   ↓
ASSIGN ACTION
   ↓
IMPROVE
   ↓
VERIFY
   ↓
EXPAND
```

Lessons from one market should improve preparation for the next.

The goal is not to create a perfect launch checklist.

The goal is to create a system that **learns from every launch.**

# 15. Redwood Metro Demonstration

The AVCLP framework will be tested using a fictional market:

**Redwood Metro, California**

Scenario RM-01 uses synthetic data to demonstrate:

- Market discovery
- Ecosystem mapping
- Partnership development
- Community introduction
- Readiness scoring
- Risk mitigation
- Launch decisions
- Controlled deployment
- Post-launch measurement

No Redwood Metro data represents a real city or real autonomous vehicle deployment.

---

# Guiding Principle

> **A new AV market should not only ask whether the vehicles are ready for the city.**
>
> **It should also ask whether the ecosystem surrounding them is ready for autonomy.**
