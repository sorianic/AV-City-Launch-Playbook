## Scenario

An autonomous vehicle operator is evaluating **Redwood Metro, California** for commercial passenger service.

The proposed initial deployment includes:

| Attribute | Scenario |
|---|---|
| Metro Population | 650,000 |
| Initial AV Fleet | 150 vehicles |
| Initial Service Area | 75 sq. miles |
| Universities | 2 |
| Community Colleges | 3 |
| Public-Safety Agencies | 6 |
| Major Hospitals | 4 |
| Airport | 1 regional airport |
| Transit | Bus + commuter rail |
| Existing AV Experience | Limited |
| Target Launch | October 2027 |

All information in this scenario is synthetic.

# Mission

Prepare Redwood Metro for commercial autonomous passenger service while exploring whether the deployment can create measurable value for:

- The AV operator
- The city
- Students
- Local workers
- Public-safety agencies
- Accessibility organizations
- Local businesses
- Transit users
- The broader community

The demonstration will apply the complete AVCLP lifecycle:

**DISCOVER → MAP → PARTNER → INTRODUCE → VERIFY → DEPLOY → MEASURE → IMPROVE**

---

# Starting Conditions

Redwood Metro presents a promising market but is not ready for commercial AV deployment.

## Mobility

The metro contains several potential AV use cases:

- First/last-mile gaps around commuter rail
- Airport transportation demand
- Major employment centers
- University districts
- Entertainment areas
- Neighborhoods with limited transportation options

## Public Safety

Police, fire, EMS, and emergency dispatch have limited experience interacting with autonomous vehicles.

No coordinated AV familiarization program currently exists.

## Workforce

The region has automotive and technical education programs but no dedicated AV operations or fleet-technology pathway.

## Accessibility

Local disability and accessibility organizations have not yet participated in the proposed deployment.

## Community

General awareness of autonomous vehicles exists, but direct experience with the technology is limited.

Community concerns and expectations have not yet been systematically collected.

## Partnerships

Potential partners exist throughout the region, but no coordinated AV ecosystem has been established.

# Initial Priority Areas

The baseline identifies five immediate priorities.

### 1. Public-Safety Preparation

Establish relationships with police, fire, EMS, dispatch, emergency management, and recovery organizations.

Develop AV familiarization and scenario-based exercises before passenger service begins.

### 2. Local Workforce Development

Identify opportunities with community colleges and workforce organizations to create exposure to AV fleet, technical, and operational careers.

### 3. Accessibility

Bring accessibility organizations into the process before service design and community introduction are finalized.

### 4. Community Introduction

Create opportunities for residents to interact with the technology before commercial service begins.

### 5. Partnership Development

Move from stakeholder identification to relationships with defined purpose, ownership, deliverables, and measurable outcomes.
