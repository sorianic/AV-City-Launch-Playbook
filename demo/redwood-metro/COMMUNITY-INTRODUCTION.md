# Redwood Metro Community Introduction Plan

**Scenario:** RM-01  
**AVCLP Phase:** T-90 — INTRODUCE  
**Status:** Synthetic / Fictional  
**Target Launch:** October 2027

> The first time a community meaningfully encounters autonomous vehicles should not have to be after commercial service begins.

---

## Objective

Prepare Redwood Metro for the introduction of autonomous passenger service through direct education, demonstrations, training, accessibility participation, workforce exposure, and structured community feedback.

This phase is not designed as a marketing campaign.

Its purpose is to create opportunities for people to:

- See the technology
- Understand its intended role
- Ask questions
- Raise concerns
- Learn how to interact with it
- Explore local career and business opportunities
- Influence issues that can still be addressed before launch

---

# AV Community Week

AVCLP proposes a coordinated five-day pre-launch event period approximately 90 days before commercial deployment.

Rather than holding unrelated outreach events, **AV Community Week** brings several parts of the local ecosystem together around the same deployment.

---

## Day 1 — Students, Education & Research

**Primary Audience:** Universities, community colleges, students, faculty

### Activities

- AV technology demonstration
- AV operations career panel
- Applied Mobility Challenge kickoff
- Student Q&A
- Workforce pathway presentation

### Intended Outcomes

- Increase student exposure to autonomous mobility
- Introduce technical and operational career paths
- Connect university projects to local mobility challenges
- Begin building a long-term education pipeline

---

## Day 2 — Public Safety

**Primary Audience:** Police, Fire, EMS, Dispatch, Emergency Management

### Activities

- Vehicle walkaround
- Emergency-access demonstration
- Disabled-vehicle scenario
- Passenger medical-response scenario
- Dispatch/escalation walkthrough
- Multi-agency tabletop exercise

### Intended Outcomes

- Increase first-responder familiarity
- Validate emergency procedures
- Identify unclear escalation paths
- Capture corrective actions before launch

### Required Output

Every identified issue receives:

**Issue → Owner → Corrective Action → Due Date → Evidence → Status**

---

## Day 3 — Accessibility & Rider Experience

**Primary Audience:** Accessibility organizations, senior mobility organizations, potential riders

### Activities

- Vehicle demonstrations
- Rider journey walkthrough
- Pick-up/drop-off review
- Accessibility workshop
- Structured feedback session

### Questions

- Where could the rider journey create barriers?
- Is information understandable?
- Are pick-up and drop-off assumptions realistic?
- What issues may not be obvious to the operator?
- What should be addressed before launch?

### Principle

> Accessibility participation should influence deployment planning, not simply validate it afterward.

---

## Day 4 — Workforce & Local Business

**Primary Audience:** Local workers, students, community colleges, workforce organizations, local businesses

### Workforce Activities

- AV career overview
- Fleet operations demonstration
- Technical pathway discussion
- Entry-level role information
- Training-provider session

### Local Business Activities

- Supplier information session
- Facilities requirements overview
- Fleet-support discussion
- Recovery/towing discussion
- Infrastructure opportunity overview

### Intended Outcomes

Create visibility into potential local opportunities without implying guaranteed employment, contracts, or partnerships.

---

## Day 5 — Community Open House

**Primary Audience:** General public

### Activities

- Vehicle demonstrations
- Technology overview
- Service-area explanation
- Safety information
- Public Q&A
- Feedback stations
- Community organization participation

The objective is not to convince every attendee to support autonomous vehicles.

The objective is to provide understandable information and create a legitimate way for questions and concerns to enter the launch process.

---

# Community Feedback System

Every event feeds the same feedback process.

```text
LISTEN
   ↓
CAPTURE
   ↓
CATEGORIZE
   ↓
PRIORITIZE
   ↓
ASSIGN OWNER
   ↓
ACTION / RESPONSE
   ↓
VERIFY
   ↓
COMMUNICATE
```

---

# Feedback Categories

| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| Safety | Concern about vehicle behavior near emergency scenes |
| Accessibility | Pick-up location creates mobility barrier |
| Service Area | Neighborhood lacks proposed coverage |
| Transit | Potential duplication or connection opportunity |
| Workforce | Questions about local hiring |
| Privacy | Questions regarding sensors or data |
| Rider Experience | Confusing pick-up process |
| Public Safety | Unclear emergency contact pathway |
| General | Technology or deployment questions |

---

# Feedback Priority

### CRITICAL

Potential safety or launch-blocking issue.

**Action:** Immediate escalation.

### HIGH

Significant issue requiring resolution or mitigation before launch.

**Action:** Assign owner and deadline.

### MODERATE

Issue requiring review but not currently launch blocking.

**Action:** Track through launch.

### INFORMATIONAL

Question, suggestion, or observation requiring response or documentation.

**Action:** Respond and record.

# T-90 Exit Criteria

Redwood Metro can move toward the T-60 Readiness Review when:

- [ ] AV Community Week has been completed
- [ ] Public-safety exercises have produced documented findings
- [ ] Accessibility feedback has been captured
- [ ] Critical feedback items have assigned owners
- [ ] High-priority issues have mitigation plans
- [ ] Workforce and student events have been completed
- [ ] Local supplier outreach has begun
- [ ] Community questions have a defined response process
- [ ] No newly identified issue requires an immediate planning hold

---

# T-90 Decision

### Current Assessment

**PROCEED TO READINESS REVIEW**

Community introduction has expanded direct participation in the Redwood Metro deployment process.

However, participation does not mean the market is ready.

Feedback from these activities now becomes evidence for the T-60 readiness assessment.

The next question is:

> **After six months of preparation, what is still not ready?**
