# Redwood Metro 90-Day Market Integration Review

**Scenario:** RM-01  
**AVCLP Phase:** +90 — MEASURE  
**Status:** Synthetic / Fictional  
**Launch:** October 2027  
**Review Period:** First 90 Days

> Deployment is not the finish line. It is where planning assumptions begin meeting real-world operations.

---

## Objective

Evaluate the first 90 days of the fictional Redwood Metro deployment across two dimensions:

1. **Operational Performance**
2. **Local Ecosystem Impact**

AVCLP asks more than whether autonomous vehicles successfully entered the market.

It also asks:

> **Did the city become more prepared and capable because the deployment happened?**

---

# Deployment Progression

Redwood Metro entered service under a **CONDITIONAL GO** recommendation.

The initial fleet was introduced in phases:

```text
LAUNCH
25 Vehicles
     ↓
+30 REVIEW
50 Vehicles
     ↓
+60 REVIEW
100 Vehicles
     ↓
+90 REVIEW
150 Vehicles
```

Expansion was based on operational and readiness evidence rather than predetermined dates alone.

---

# +30 Day Review

## Focus

Stabilize initial service and validate assumptions from pre-launch planning.

### Key Findings

- Initial fleet operations remained stable
- Public-safety contact pathways functioned as intended
- Several rider-experience issues were identified
- Community questions continued after launch
- Workforce demand was slightly higher than projected
- Accessibility feedback identified two additional improvement opportunities

### Decision

**CONTINUE CONTROLLED EXPANSION**

Fleet progression authorized from 25 to 50 vehicles.

---

# +60 Day Review

## Focus

Evaluate whether operational support and the local ecosystem can support broader service.

### Key Findings

- Fleet-support processes improved
- Workforce qualification increased
- First-responder interactions produced no unresolved procedural gaps
- Community concerns became more focused on service availability and rider experience
- Accessibility recommendations continued through corrective-action review
- Local supplier participation increased

### Decision

**CONTINUE EXPANSION WITH MONITORING**

Fleet progression authorized toward 100 vehicles.

# Lessons Learned

## 1. Early Stakeholder Mapping Matters

Several launch dependencies would have been harder to address if stakeholder engagement began immediately before deployment.

## 2. Public-Safety Familiarization Should Occur Before Launch

Pre-launch exercises created an opportunity to identify questions before real-world interactions occurred.

## 3. Workforce Readiness Can Become a Deployment Constraint

Vehicle readiness does not automatically mean workforce readiness.

## 4. Accessibility Requires a Feedback Loop

Pre-launch workshops were useful, but real-world service still produced additional observations.

## 5. Community Engagement Does Not End at Launch

Questions changed after commercial service began.

Pre-launch concerns focused heavily on understanding the technology.

Post-launch feedback became more focused on actual service experience.

## 6. Partnership Value Should Be Measured

The number of partnerships matters less than whether those relationships produced useful outcomes.
